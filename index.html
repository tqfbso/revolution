      The enemy base is off the map until one of your units sees it: scout. A battle is set up on
      the New battle screen (main menu, Skirmish): map, armies, time, weather and opponent. The menu
      button (top right, three lines) has sound, graphics, Restart battle and Main menu.

      Versus (2 to 4 players): the same invitation, with Game set to Versus in the lobby. Red
      against blue and no computer; everyone picks a side, players on one side share its army (2 v
      2, a duel, or uneven sides), and each side sees only what its own troops see

      Co-op (2 to 4 players): main menu, "Co-op with friends" gives one link to send to everyone;
      the host presses Start when all are in. Whoever opens it commands the same army with you
      against the computer. Z rings the point under your pointer on every minimap and tells everyone
      its grid square; the cyan box on the minimap is where your partner is looking. Pause and speed
      are shared.

      Map grid: the minimap is ruled in squares, A to H across and 1 to 8 down ("armour to C4"). K
      (or the Grid button) draws the same squares and their names on the battlefield.

      Engineers and trucks: B (or Repair / fortify), then right click a building, to mend it;
      engineers then fortify it. Engineers clear street rubble: right click it.

      Economy: select trucks, right click a stockpile (tan marker) or an oil well (amber) to haul;
      the buttons at the bottom build. Hold Shift while placing to put down several: the trucks
      build them one after the other, in the order placed, and a line shows what is still to come
      (Shift+right click a building of yours adds it to the list). Click one of your buildings to
      produce units.

      Supplies run out, wells pump slowly (hold more wells), towns pay credits for technology.

      Supply: income is generous, keeping an army fed is the limit. Troops are in supply within 150
      m of a depot or headquarters, a little past the edge of a town you hold, and within 50 m of a
      truck that is itself within 260 m of one of those (the lighter ground with the green edge on
      the minimap). Beyond it they have rations for two minutes; after that they fire at half rate
      and vehicles crawl, until they are back in supply. In supply and out of the fight, troops
      mend. A long push needs a truck with it, a forward depot, or the next town. Upkeep (top bar):
      every army point above 60 costs supplies each minute

      Production (P, or the button at the top): every factory on one screen. Order 20 squads and all
      your barracks share the work; each unit is paid for as it starts, so you can order ahead of
      your money. Keep: that many are kept in the field, losses replaced by themselves. Send to: new
      units of that kind go straight to a town instead of the factory's rally point

      Commanders: select troops and press N, and they get a commander (Alpha to Hotel, eight at
      most). The board top right lists them all the time: mission, objective and what it is doing,
      grid square, strength and number of units (M hides and shows the board). Click its row and
      give it a mission, then click the map (or the minimap) for the place. Attack: it forms up,
      marches as one body, closes up outside the place where the enemy cannot see it (for 20 seconds
      at most), assaults with smoke and guns, holds what it took, and breaks off and waits for
      reinforcements when it is losing. Move (or T with its troops selected): goes to the place and
      stands there, without stopping to fight on the way, going into houses or falling back.
      Concentrate (or R with its troops selected): everything it has onto the exact spot you click,
      each unit at its own best speed, with no forming up and no waiting outside; the last stretch
      is fought, and they stay massed there until you give the mission again. Hold: stays to the
      last, infantry in the houses. Defend: holds, goes out to meet an enemy nearby, gives ground to
      one far too strong and comes back. Patrol: back and forth between where it stood and the
      place. Screen: a thin line of lookouts that pulls back from anything strong. Replace losses:
      what it loses is ordered again and sent to it. Production's "Send to" can also send every new
      unit of a kind to a commander. An order you give a unit yourself takes it out of its
      commander's hands (select it and press + Add on the board to put it back). The board also
      counts idle units and can hand them to the nearest commander

      You command red. Drag: select · Double click: all of that type · Shift: add · Ctrl+A: all

      Right click: move (breaks off a fight) · Right click an enemy: attack it · C then click or
      Alt+right click: attack-move · Shift+right click: way point (the troops go there after where

      Right click a building: infantry moves in · Ctrl+right click a building or bridge: shell it

      Engineers: O (Demolish), then right click a building or bridge (charges), a wood (they fell a
      patch, which lets vehicles through and clears the view) or a wall, wire or rubble. People
      still in a house that is brought down are killed, and you pay for them

      Right click a building the enemy holds: infantry storms it, room by room. Bring three men for
      every defender, or fewer if a tank has breached a wall first or smoke covers the way in.
      Engineers are worth four riflemen in the assault

      Buildings come apart slowly: damaged, then a gutted shell, then a ruin, and each still
      shelters infantry. Tank guns breach the wall they hit (a hole the men behind are exposed
      through); only artillery and engineers' charges bring a building down fast. A ruin cannot be
      destroyed

      Ctrl+right click the ground: guns shell that spot (X to cease fire) · F: guns lay smoke at the
      pointer, which blinds both sides

      Y: see through the buildings between you and the street you are looking at (close up only; off
      until you press it, and remembered)

      Weather (New battle screen): in rain everyone sees 85% as far, in snow 80%, in fog 55%, scouts
      and drones included. No dust in rain or snow

      Free-for-all (New battle screen, Armies: 3 or 4): every army for itself on the four corners
      map. You are red, bottom left; blue, gold and green are computers that fight you and each
      other. You win when the last enemy headquarters has fallen

      Armies (New battle screen, Your army and Enemy): the Western alliance has fewer, better and
      dearer units, the toughest tank, long sight and a precise long gun, but only a light jammer (a
      smaller ring, dearer). Russia has cheap fast-firing tanks, rocket batteries, long-armed air
      defence and the strongest jammer, but no transport helicopter. China has the cheapest
      infantry, swarm drones at well under the usual price, fast wheeled carriers that seat three
      squads and hardy engineers, but no paratroopers

      Night battles (New battle screen, Time: night): troops see six tenths as far, scouts and their
      drone as far as by day. Whatever is lit is seen at daytime range: a unit that has just fired,
      anyone near a burning vehicle, and everything under a flare. I: the nearest selected gun puts
      a flare over the pointer for 30 seconds (it lights your own troops too)

      Field works: select trucks or engineers (Barracks), pick a work at the bottom and drag a line
      on the ground. Walls and gates stop everything (gates open for you), barbed wire stops men,
      tank obstacles stop vehicles, trenches and sandbags are cover: right click them with infantry

      Right click an enemy work: tanks and guns shoot it down, engineers blow it up. Tanks drive
      over wire. Units ordered past a wall with no way round breach it themselves if they can

      Right click a carrier (or L with squads and carriers selected): mount up · U: dismount

      Drones fly over everything; a red ring on the ground is an enemy jammer: drones die inside
