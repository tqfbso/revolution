      The enemy base is off the map until one of your units sees it: scout. The Menu (top right)
      sets the opponent and starts a new game.

      Co-op: Menu, "Co-op: invite a partner" gives a link to send. Whoever opens it commands the
      same army with you against the computer. Z rings the point under your pointer on both
      minimaps; the cyan box on the minimap is where your partner is looking. Pause and speed are
      shared.

      Engineers and trucks: B (or Repair / fortify), then right click a building, to mend it;
      engineers then fortify it. Engineers clear street rubble: right click it.

      Economy: select trucks, right click a stockpile (tan marker) or an oil well (amber) to haul;
      the buttons at the bottom build. Click one of your buildings to produce units.

      Supplies run out, wells pump slowly (hold more wells), towns pay credits for technology.

      Supply: income is generous, keeping an army fed is the limit. Troops are in supply within 150
      m of a depot or headquarters, a little past the edge of a town you hold, and within 50 m of a
      truck that is itself within 260 m of one of those (the lighter ground with the green edge on
      the minimap). Beyond it they have rations for two minutes; after that they fire at half rate
      and vehicles crawl, until they are back in supply. In supply and out of the fight, troops
      mend. A long push needs a truck with it, a forward depot, or the next town. Upkeep (top bar):
      every army point above 60 costs supplies each minute

      Production (P, or the button at the top): every factory on one screen. Order 20 squads and all
      your barracks share the work; each unit is paid for as it starts, so you can order ahead of
      your money. Keep: that many are kept in the field, losses replaced by themselves. Send to: new
      units of that kind go straight to a town instead of the factory's rally point

      Commanders (M, or the button at the top): select troops and press N, and they get a commander
      (Alpha to Hotel, eight at most). Open its row and give it a mission, then click the map (or
      the minimap) for the place. Attack: it forms up, marches as one body, assaults with smoke and
      guns, holds what it took, and breaks off and waits for reinforcements when it is losing. Hold:
      stays to the last, infantry in the houses. Defend: holds, goes out to meet an enemy nearby,
      gives ground to one far too strong and comes back. Patrol: back and forth between where it
      stood and the place. Screen: a thin line of lookouts that pulls back from anything strong.
      Replace losses: what it loses is ordered again and sent to it. Production's "Send to" can also
      send every new unit of a kind to a commander. An order you give a unit yourself takes it out
      of its commander's hands (select it and press + Add on the board to put it back). The board
      also counts idle units and can hand them to the nearest commander

      You command red. Drag: select · Double click: all of that type · Shift: add · Ctrl+A: all

      Right click: move (breaks off a fight) · Right click an enemy: attack it · C then click or
      Alt+right click: attack-move · X: stop · H: hold position · G: hold fire · . (full stop): idle
      trucks · Esc: clear

      Right click a building: infantry moves in · Ctrl+right click a building or bridge: shell it

      Right click a building the enemy holds: infantry storms it, room by room. Bring three men for
      every defender, or fewer if a tank has breached a wall first or smoke covers the way in.
      Engineers are worth four riflemen in the assault

      Buildings come apart slowly: damaged, then a gutted shell, then a ruin, and each still
      shelters infantry. Tank guns breach the wall they hit (a hole the men behind are exposed
      through); only artillery and engineers' charges bring a building down fast. A ruin cannot be
      destroyed

      Ctrl+right click the ground: guns shell that spot (X to cease fire) · F: guns lay smoke at the
      pointer, which blinds both sides

      Field works: select trucks or engineers (Barracks), pick a work at the bottom and drag a line
      on the ground. Walls and gates stop everything (gates open for you), barbed wire stops men,
      tank obstacles stop vehicles, trenches and sandbags are cover: right click them with infantry
      to man them

      Right click an enemy work: tanks and guns shoot it down, engineers blow it up. Tanks drive
      over wire. Units ordered past a wall with no way round breach it themselves if they can

      Right click a carrier (or L with squads and carriers selected): mount up · U: dismount

      Drones fly over everything; a red ring on the ground is an enemy jammer: drones die inside
      it

      V with engineers selected: leave a surveillance camera where they stand (20 supplies, 12 at
      most). It watches a small circle, raises a contact alert, and the enemy cannot see or shoot
      it. An enemy jammer blinds it (its ring turns red) and gives it away; then any enemy ground
      unit that walks up pulls it down. Your jammer does the same to theirs: found cameras get a red
      mark

      Ctrl or Alt/Option + 0..9: save group (on a Mac use Option: Ctrl + number belongs to the
      system), or click + Group · with Shift as well: add the selection to that group · 0..9: recall
      (Shift: add to the selection) · twice: jump to it. Groups show as numbered chips above the
      command card; click one to select it · Minimap: click to look, right click to order

      Space: pause. Orders given while paused all start when you resume, so you can plan a line of
      works or an attack in peace. Speed 0.75x slows the whole game a little

      Alerts: when you are attacked off screen, or a recon drone finds enemy troops, a banner, a
      ping on the minimap and an arrow at the screen edge show where. Backspace (or a click on any
      of them) takes you there; Backspace again goes to the one before

      WASD / arrows / screen edge: pan · Wheel: zoom · Q/E or middle drag: rotate

      Every order with a key also has a button at the bottom when the right units are selected;
      hover over a button to read what it does. F3 (or a click on the frame-rate line): the full
      performance readout
